Features and Usage Guide
A full description of every feature, a step-by-step workflow for each one, and worked examples. Your photo is processed entirely in your browser and never leaves your device.
1. What you can do with this software
Tab	What it does
Adjust	Fine-tune light, colour and effects with 10 sliders
Orient	Rotate, flip, straighten and crop
Looks	9 ready-made filters
BG blur	Blur the background (circle, band or brush)
Export	Save in 8 formats, or compress to a target file size
Also included: Undo/Redo, Hold to compare, Reset all, a Light/Dark theme and keyboard shortcuts.
Core rule: every edit is recalculated from the original photo, so moving a slider many times never degrades quality, and the original file stays untouched until you download.
2. Quick start: the standard workflow
1.	Open a photo (Open image, drag and drop, or Ctrl+V).
2.	In the Orient tab, rotate, straighten and crop to get the framing right.
3.	In the Adjust tab, correct light and colour.
4.	In the Looks tab, pick a filter you like.
5.	If needed, use the BG blur tab to blur the background.
6.	Hold Hold to compare to check the result against the original.
7.	In the Export tab, choose a format and size, then press Download.
Every step can be undone, so experiment freely. 
3. Opening a photo
What it does: loads a photo into the editor. Any image your browser can display works, including JPEG, PNG, WebP and GIF.
Workflow (use any one):
1.	Click Open image at the top or in the middle of the canvas, or press Ctrl+O.
2.	Or drag the file onto the canvas and drop it.
3.	Or copy an image anywhere and press Ctrl+V to paste it.
4.	The photo's size (for example 1600 × 1066 px) appears at the bottom right of the canvas.
Opening a new photo clears all earlier edits and history.
4. Adjust: light, colour and effects
What it does: 10 sliders arranged in three groups. Changes show live in the preview.
Group	Slider	Range	Effect
Light	Exposure	-100 to 100	Multiplies the overall light (-100 is about half, +100 about double)
Light	Brightness	-100 to 100	Simple lightening or darkening
Light	Contrast	-100 to 100	Difference between lights and darks
Colour	Saturation	-100 to 100	Colour intensity (-100 is nearly black and white)
Colour	Hue	-180 to 180	Rotates all colours around the colour wheel
Colour	Warmth	-100 to 100	From cool (blue) to warm (orange)
Colour	Tint	-100 to 100	From green towards magenta
Effects	Sharpen	0 to 100	Increases edge detail
Effects	Blur	0 to 20	Blurs the whole photo
Effects	Vignette	0 to 100	Darkens the corners
Workflow:
1.	Open the Adjust tab.
2.	Start with Light: raise Exposure if the photo is too dark, then use Contrast to bring back depth.
3.	Move to Colour: use Saturation and Warmth to set the mood.
4.	Finish with Effects: a little Sharpen, and Vignette if you want it.
5.	To undo one slider, double-click it and it returns to its default.
6.	Use Hold to compare to check against the original.
Tips: keep Sharpen low (20 to 40); too much makes the photo look gritty. Blur softens the whole photo. To blur only the background, use BG blur instead.
5. Orient: rotate, flip, straighten and crop
What it does: fixes the direction and framing of the photo.
Tool	What it does
Rotate left / right	Rotates by 90°
Flip horizontal / vertical	Mirrors the photo (the button turns blue while active)
Straighten	Fine rotation from -15° to +15° to level a tilted horizon
Crop	Crops to Original, 1:1, 4:3, 3:2, 16:9 or 4:5
Workflow: straighten a tilted photo
1.	Open the Orient tab.
2.	If the photo is sideways, press Rotate left or Rotate right.
3.	Drag the Straighten slider until the horizon or a wall line is level. The photo zooms in slightly so no empty corners appear.
4.	Double-click the slider to reset it to 0.
Workflow: crop for social media
1.	In the Crop section, pick a ratio: 1:1 or 4:5 for Instagram posts, 16:9 for YouTube thumbnails.
2.	Check the new size at the bottom right of the canvas (the crop is taken from the centre).
3.	Press Original to go back to the full frame.
Keep in mind: the crop always comes from the centre and cannot be moved. The background-blur area stays correctly placed after you rotate, flip or crop.
6. Looks: ready-made filters
What it does: changes the mood of the whole photo in one click. Each filter thumbnail shows a preview of your own photo.
Filter	Style
Original	No filter
Vivid	Bright, rich colour
Fade	Soft, faded vintage
Warm / Cool	Warm or cool tone
Mono	Black and white
Noir	Dramatic, high-contrast black and white
Sepia	Old-fashioned brown tint
Punch	Strong contrast
Workflow:
1.	Open the Looks tab.
2.	Click any thumbnail; the active one gets a coloured border.
3.	If you don't like it, click another one, or Original to switch it off.
4.	The Adjust sliders still work on top of a filter, so you can combine the two to fine-tune the result.
7. BG blur: blur the background
What it does: keeps the main subject (a person, pet or object) sharp and blurs everything behind it, like a portrait-mode camera.
Method	When to use it	Internet needed
Auto-detect subject (AI)	Finds a person, pet or object on its own	First use only
Circle focus	A round sharp area in the middle	No
Band focus	A horizontal sharp strip across the photo	No
Brush	Paint the area by hand to fix details	No
Settings:
•	Background blur (0 to 100): how strong the blur is. It starts at 40 the first time you turn it on.
•	Edge softness (0 to 30, default 8): how soft the border between sharp and blurred areas is.
•	Focus size (10 to 100, default 50): the size of the circle or band. It only affects those two methods.
•	Clear background blur: removes the blur completely.
Workflow A: portrait with AI
1.	Open the BG blur tab.
2.	Choose a Subject type: Person / portrait for people, or Objects, pets, people for pets and things.
3.	Press Auto-detect subject. The first time, it downloads the AI model, which takes a few seconds.
4.	Use the Background blur slider to set how strong the blur is.
5.	If the wrong area is blurred, press Swap sharp / blur.
6.	If the edges look jagged, raise Edge softness.
7.	Fix any remaining mistakes with the brush (Workflow D).
Workflow B: circle focus without internet
1.	Press Circle focus.
2.	Choose the Move focus tool, then click or drag on the photo to put the round area over your subject.
3.	Use Focus size to set the size of the round area.
4.	Use Background blur to make the blur stronger or weaker.
Workflow C: band focus
Good for landscapes or a miniature, tilt-shift look.
1.	Press Band focus.
2.	Use Move focus to slide the sharp strip up or down.
3.	Use Focus size to set the height of the strip.
Workflow D: fine-tune with the brush
1.	Press Show blur area. The blurred part turns red (this switches on automatically when you pick a brush).
2.	Keep sharp brush: paint over parts that were blurred by mistake, such as hair, ears or hands.
3.	Blur more brush: paint over parts that should be blurred but stayed sharp.
4.	Change Brush size (8 to 160) to make the brush smaller or larger. A circle shows the brush size over the photo.
5.	Press the same tool button again to switch the tool off.
Keep in mind: moving the focus or changing Focus size redraws the circle or band and erases earlier brush work, so set the focus first and use the brush last. The blur area stays in the right place after you rotate, flip or crop.
8. Compare, Undo/Redo, Reset and theme
•	Hold to compare: hold the button to see the original photo (rotation and crop stay as they are); release to bring your edits back. Holding the \ key does the same.
•	Undo / Redo: up to 40 steps, including every brush stroke. Shortcuts are Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z.
•	Reset all: clears every edit and returns to the original photo. You can undo this too.
•	Theme button (sun or moon, top right): switches between Light and Dark and remembers your choice.
9. Export: formats and size
Format	When to use it	Notes
JPEG	Regular photos, works everywhere	No transparency (transparent areas become white); Quality slider
PNG	Logos, screenshots, transparent backgrounds	Lossless, larger files
WebP	Websites	Smallest files; Quality slider
AVIF	Even smaller files	Not every browser can create it; the option is greyed out if yours can't
GIF	Simple, small images	Up to 256 colours; you can pick the number of Colours
BMP	Special programs	Not compressed; very large files
ICO	Website icons (favicons)	Up to 256 × 256 px
PDF	Turning a photo into a PDF	One page the size of the photo; no transparency
Workflow:
1.	Open the Export tab.
2.	Pick a Format. A short note about that format appears below it.
3.	For JPEG, WebP, AVIF or PDF, set the Quality. For GIF, choose the number of Colours.
4.	If needed, use Longest side to shrink the photo (3840 down to 480 px). The final size shows at the bottom right of the canvas.
5.	Press Download.
6.	The saved file name (photo-name-edited.ext), file size and dimensions appear below the button.
10. Compress: hit a target file size
What it does: you choose a size in KB, and the app lowers the quality (and the dimensions if needed) until the file fits. It works with any format.
Workflow:
1.	In the Export tab, pick a Format. WebP or JPEG give the smallest files.
2.	Turn on Compress to a target file size, or press a preset from 20 KB to 1 MB (a preset turns the switch on for you).
3.	For a different size, type the KB value in Target size (2 to 5000).
4.	Choose a Priority: Keep quality lowers the dimensions to protect quality, while Keep dimensions keeps the size and lowers quality.
5.	Press Download. The app tries several settings and keeps the best result that fits.
6.	The result appears below, for example 19.7 KB · 1600 × 1066 px · quality 60%.
7.	If the target can't be reached, the app tells you. Then try a larger target or choose WebP or JPEG.
Format	How it gets smaller
JPEG, WebP, AVIF, PDF	Lowers quality, and the dimensions if needed
PNG	Reduces colour levels, and the dimensions if needed
GIF	Reduces the number of colours, and the dimensions if needed
ICO	Stays within 256 px and reduces colours
BMP	Cannot be compressed, so only the dimensions shrink
Results depend on how detailed the photo is. With PNG and BMP, a small target shrinks the dimensions a lot, so choose WebP or JPEG when you want a small file.

12. Worked examples: complete workflows
A) Profile picture under 20 KB
1.	Open the photo.
2.	Orient → Crop → 1:1.
3.	BG blur → Auto-detect subject (Person / portrait).
4.	Export → Format WebP → 20 KB preset → Download.
B) Portrait with a blurred background
1.	Open the photo and set Exposure and Warmth in Adjust.
2.	BG blur → Auto-detect → Swap and brush if needed.
3.	Soften the border with Edge softness.
4.	Export → JPEG, Quality 92 → Download.
C) Website banner
1.	Open the photo, then Orient → Crop → 16:9.
2.	Pick the Vivid filter in Looks.
3.	Export → WebP, Longest side 1920 px, switch on compression, 200 KB preset → Download.
D) Website icon
1.	Open the photo, then Orient → Crop → 1:1.
2.	Export → Format ICO → Download.
E) Turn a scanned page into a PDF
1.	Open the photo, then Orient → Straighten to level the page.
2.	Adjust → raise Contrast.
3.	Export → Format PDF (switch on compression if there is a size limit) → Download.
13. Limitations and troubleshooting
Problem	What to do
Auto-detect does not work	It needs internet the first time. Without it, use Circle focus, Band focus or the brush.
The wrong area is blurred	Press Swap sharp / blur, then fix the rest with the brush.
The AVIF or WebP option is greyed out	Your browser can't create that format. Choose JPEG or PNG, or use Chrome, Edge or Firefox.
The target size was not reached	Choose a larger target, or pick WebP or JPEG.
Editing is slow on a big photo	Shrink it with Longest side, or start with a smaller photo.
The crop always comes from the centre	The crop area can't be moved yet. Rotate or flip before cropping.
GIF shows bands of colour	Set Colours to 256, or use JPEG or WebP for photos.
Opening a new photo loses your edits	Edits are not saved before you download them, and refreshing the page also loses them, so download first.
Transparent areas turn white in JPEG	Use PNG, WebP or GIF to keep transparency.
